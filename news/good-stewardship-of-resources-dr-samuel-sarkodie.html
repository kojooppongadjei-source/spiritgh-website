Earth Is God’s, Not Ours: Dr Samuel Sarkodie Calls for Collective Stewardship

By Paa Kwesi Enyan · 13 November 2026

Individuals, the Church and government must all take responsibility for protecting the earth and its resources, says Transformative Stewardship Africa’s Dr Samuel Sarkodie.

The earth and its resources are not possessions to exploit at will, but gifts God has entrusted to people to manage. That was the message from Dr Samuel Sarkodie on Spirit FM’s Inspirational Morning Drive, hosted by Akwasi Antwi Fredua and Paa Kwesi Enyan.

Dr Sarkodie represents Transformative Stewardship Africa (TSA), a Christian non-governmental organisation focused on advocacy and mindset change. TSA works with students, institutions, churches and the public on nation-building, stewardship, resource use and environmental sustainability.

Speaking as part of the programme’s Stewardship Series, on the theme “Saving the World Through Good Stewardship of Resources,” he said good stewardship starts with recognising God as the owner of creation.

“Human beings are not owners, but God is the only one who owns the earth and all the resources that are in it.”

Stewardship, he explained, means using resources wisely and sustainably, in ways that honour the Creator. People must not abuse what has been entrusted to them, and must remember that their actions affect both the present and the future.

A growing threat to the earth’s resources

Dr Sarkodie warned that the rate at which natural resources are being depleted should worry everyone. Some plants and organisms are disappearing or declining because of human activity, pollution and the destruction of ecosystems.

He cautioned against assuming that underground water can simply replace polluted surface water. The two are connected, he said, and damage to one affects the other. He called for wiser use of resources: consuming less, reusing materials and recycling.

The Church must lead

Dr Sarkodie said the stewardship mandate is rooted in Genesis chapters 1 and 2, where God gives humanity responsibility for the earth. Christians who understand this from scripture, he argued, should set the example in caring for creation.

Asked whether the Church had done enough, he answered candidly: “Largely I would say NO.”

Non-governmental organisations have mostly taken the lead, he observed. He challenged churches to become more intentional, to make care for the environment part of their practical witness, and to be seen championing “the salvation and the redemption of creation of the earth.”

Responsibility begins with the individual

Governments, international bodies and NGOs all have roles to play, Dr Sarkodie said. But laws and policies only work when citizens change their attitudes and commit to them.

He warned against dismissing small actions as insignificant. “A work that is to be done by somebody is done by nobody,” he said, urging listeners not to wait for someone else to act.

Using littering as an example, he noted that a place becomes dirty through many small acts, such as people carelessly dropping sachet wrappers. In the same way, consistent good choices can turn things around. “Your contribution can reverse the trend in a positive direction,” he said.

A mandate to cultivate and keep

Returning to Genesis, Dr Sarkodie pointed to Genesis 2:15, where humanity is told to tend and keep the garden. The mandate, he said, is not permission to exploit creation but a call to cultivate it and care for it so it can flourish.

Dominion over the earth is not a licence for destruction, he added. People are custodians who must manage creation responsibly, knowing it does not belong to them alone and that others will come after them.

The best inheritance is a safe earth

On legacy, Dr Sarkodie quoted Proverbs 13:22: “A good person leaves an inheritance for their children’s children.” A good steward, he said, is a transgenerational thinker who weighs how today’s decisions will affect generations to come.

Inheritance should not be limited to land, houses, money or education, he said. He asked listeners what material possessions would be worth if their grandchildren had no clean water, healthy soil, good food or a working natural environment.

“The best inheritance is a safe world, a safe earth that sustains.”

Citizens must hold leaders accountable

Dr Sarkodie urged citizens to speak up for responsible leadership in managing natural resources. He called for a national conversation that rises above partisan divisions and promotes peaceful, lawful civic engagement.

Individuals, he said, should keep calling on leaders to protect the country’s water bodies, forests, farmlands and biodiversity. The responsibility is shared: citizens must act responsibly in their own lives while holding institutions and leaders to account.

The central message of the discussion was that caring for creation is faith in action. Good stewardship takes more than words. It calls for responsible daily choices, collective commitment and a willingness to protect the earth for those living today and those who will inherit it tomorrow.
